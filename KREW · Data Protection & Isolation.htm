Section 1 · The promise

How we keep client data separate, and client work private

Authors: CTO (Sheldon) & CLO (Louis) · approved by CEO (Michael)

Two promises sit underneath everything KREW sells. First, no client can ever see another client''s data. Second, KREW itself cannot read a client''s sealed work — not the engineers, not the agents'' operators, not the Chairman. This note explains exactly how each promise is kept, and — just as importantly — where the honest boundary of each one lies. A guarantee you cannot describe precisely is not a guarantee.

The two guarantees

G1
Client-to-client isolation. No client can ever see another client''s data. This is enforced in the database (row-level security), not in the user interface. A stolen client token still reaches only that client''s own rows — there is no query, no screen, and no API path that crosses from one client''s world into another''s.
G2
Operator-blind on sealed work. For work a client chooses to seal, KREW humans — including the Chairman, with full dashboard and direct database access — cannot read the content. This is enforced by cryptography, not policy: the content is encrypted in the client''s own browser before it ever reaches KREW infrastructure, and the key never leaves the client''s device. Our dashboard, our admin SQL, and our database backups all hold ciphertext only.
Why the distinction matters. G1 is absolute and automatic — it protects every client, all the time, with no action required of anyone. G2 is the stronger promise (it shuts out even us), and it applies wherever a client has sealed their work. The rest of this note is how both are built, and the one unavoidable limit we are honest about: the moment an agent actually works on data, that data must be readable. We design everything around making that moment as small, contained, and accountable as possible.

The architecture below was proven first on our own platform (the Boardroom and QMS), under the principle that KREW is its own first customer. We do not ask clients to trust a diagram; we run on the same controls ourselves.

Section 2 · Defence in depth

The five layers that protect client data

Author: CTO (Sheldon)

No single control is trusted to carry the whole promise. Five layers stack, each defeating a different class of failure:

#LayerMechanismWhat it defeats
1Tenant isolationRow-level security (RLS) on every table; a row is visible only to its owner, those it is explicitly shared with, or active members of its organisation. Security-definer helper functions perform the checks so policies cannot recurse or be bypassed.Cross-client reads, stolen tokens, API probing
2Sealed contentClient-side AES-256-GCM encryption; the key is derived in the client''s browser (PBKDF2-SHA256, 310,000 iterations, per-item salt); a verifier blob proves the key is correct without exposing the content.Operator reads, dashboard browsing, backup leaks, a subpoenaed disk
3Time-boxed accessEvery membership carries an expires_at; RLS checks expiry at query time, so access ends automatically — no human has to remember to revoke it.Forgotten offboarding, lingering accounts
4Break-glass support grantsIf KREW needs access to help, the client grants it: a time-limited (24h), reason-tagged, fully audited, revocable grant — and it can only be granted to a KREW account. No standing access exists.Silent or open-ended operator access
5Audit & hardened accessEvery grant, membership change and approval is written to an append-only audit log; access functions are security definer and authenticated-only; anonymous access gets nothing.Untraceable access, privilege drift
One honest mechanism note. Layers 1, 3, 4 and 5 govern who is allowed in — they are policy enforced by the database. Layer 2 is different: it removes the content from view even for someone with full database rights. That is why sealing is the layer that delivers G2, and the others deliver G1.

Section 3 · No mixing, and the honest boundary

How separation is enforced — and the one limit we name

Author: CTO (Sheldon)

How "no mixing" is actually enforced

Each logo client is one organisation; each human belongs to exactly one organisation. Every record carries its organisation, and the database refuses to return a record to anyone outside it. Concretely:

  • Membership, not assumption. A user sees a client''s data only if they are an active member of that organisation. Membership is a row, checked on every single query.
  • Offboarding is instant and total. Deactivating a member flips one flag; the very next query returns zero rows for them, and their item-level memberships are removed in the same action.
  • Enforced in the database, not the screen. Hiding a button is not security. The protection lives in RLS, so even a hand-crafted API call with a valid token cannot reach another organisation''s rows.
  • The CSM onboarding ("sealing") flow grants a named human time-limited access to one client only, recorded with a role and an expiry; the grant lapses on its own.
A client''s private knowledge (the Enterprise Context, Layer 4 of the agent model) is stored per-client under this isolation and is never mixed into another client''s context, never used to train a shared model, and never visible to anyone outside that client''s organisation. Separation is a property of the data model itself.

The one boundary we are honest about

The inference moment. For an agent to work on a client''s data, the data must be readable at that instant — a model cannot reason over ciphertext. So at the moment of processing, plaintext exists transiently in memory. This is unavoidable for any AI system, and we will not pretend otherwise. What we control is everything around it: processing happens in the EU; the data is not retained beyond the working session; it is never used to train any model; and it stays inside the client''s isolated context throughout. Sealed content is decrypted only on the client''s device or transiently for an authorised action — never browsable at rest by KREW.
Service-role reality. KREW''s own administrative database access (service role) bypasses RLS by design — that is what admin access is for, and it is how we build and debug. It means that for un-sealed content, the operator could technically read it. That is precisely why sensitive client work is sealed: sealing is the only layer that shuts out the operator too. Our standing posture: client work that warrants G2 is sealed; KREW''s own internal cases are typically unsealed so we can run the business.

Section 4 · GDPR & compliance

The legal posture around the architecture

Author: CLO (Louis)

The architecture above is what makes the legal commitments real rather than aspirational. The compliance posture:

Roles & residency

  • Controller / processor. For a client''s own data, the client is the data controller and KREW is the processor, acting only on the client''s documented instructions.
  • EU establishment & residency. KREW.CLOUD EUROPE (Ireland) is the EU operating entity; client data is hosted in the EU (Ireland / Frankfurt). It does not leave the EU in the ordinary course.

The GDPR principles, mapped to mechanism

PrincipleHow KREW meets it
Purpose limitation & instructionsKREW processes only to deliver the contracted service, on the controller''s instructions, under a Data Processing Agreement (part of the contract suite).
Data minimisationInternal learning signals are anonymised at ingestion — names and identifiers stripped before storage — so the agent-improvement loop holds no personal data.
Confidentiality & integrityThe five layers in Section 2: isolation, sealing, time-boxed access, audited break-glass, encryption in transit and at rest.
Sub-processorsThe foundation-model provider, database and hosting providers are engaged under terms that exclude using submitted data to train their models; the list is disclosed and kept current, with EU transfer safeguards where applicable.
Data-subject rightsAccess, export and erasure paths are built in. Sealed data can be rendered permanently unreadable by destroying its key (crypto-shredding) — a clean, verifiable erasure.
Breach responseThe audit log supports forensics; a personal-data breach is notifiable within 72 hours (GDPR Art. 33), with the controller informed without undue delay.
No training on client data. A client''s work is never used to train shared models and never enters another client''s context. This holds at the KREW layer and is required of every sub-processor by contract — and re-verified, not assumed.

Beyond GDPR

Confidentiality is also contractual (confidentiality terms, the DPA, EU residency) — belt and braces with the architecture. Responsible-AI posture follows the Code of Conduct (agent Layer 1) and the rule "agents advise, humans decide": a human owns any decision of consequence, and the EU AI Act''s transparency expectations are met by open AI disclosure on agent output.

Section 5 · What we can honestly promise

The commitments, and the governance behind them

Authors: CTO (Sheldon) & CLO (Louis)

The commitments, stated plainly

  • We will not mix client data. Categorical, and enforced by the database for every query — not a policy we ask you to trust, a rule the system cannot break.
  • We cannot read your sealed work. Cryptographically true: the key is yours and never reaches us. The only plaintext that ever exists is the transient moment an agent you authorised does the work you asked for — in the EU, never retained, never used to train anything.
  • Access to help is yours to grant. KREW holds no standing access to your world. Support access is client-granted, time-boxed, audited, and revocable.
  • Your data stays in the EU, and leaves when you say so. Export and erasure are built in; erasure of sealed data is a key-destruction away.
On "100% certain". In security, certainty is earned by naming the boundary, not by hiding it. We are 100% certain on cross-client separation (G1) because the database makes mixing impossible. We are operator-blind on sealed work (G2) by cryptography. The single honest exception — the inference moment — is unavoidable for any AI, and we have made it as small, contained, EU-resident and accountable as it can be. That precision is the guarantee.

Governance

  • We run on it ourselves. These controls protect KREW''s own Boardroom and QMS first — our own first customer.
  • Auditable by design. Grants, memberships and approvals are logged; nothing material happens without a trace.
  • Reviewed on change. A new foundation model, sub-processor or data flow triggers a CTO + CLO review before it goes live.
  • Registered. This note is a controlled QMS document (KREW-OPS-010); revisions are versioned and superseded under the QMS, never edited silently.
