Section 1 · Executive Summary

KREW.CLOUD Solutions — the firm that does the work

Owner: CEO (Michael) · reviewed by CFO (Bud) and CLO (Louis)

KREW.CLOUD Solutions is a specialist AI transformation consultancy. It helps Norwegian companies redesign work using agentic AI — not by selling subscriptions, but by delivering the transformation itself. Slow-burn, high-margin, and structurally defensible. Its edge is direct access to 300 KREW agents at internal transfer pricing no competitor can match. Maximum 30 humans. Target: NOK 200M annual revenue by Year 3.

The thesis

Norway has roughly 25,000 companies with 50+ employees. Most run expensive, hard-to-staff knowledge functions. Agentic AI can replace or dramatically augment large portions of that work — and the market for AI transformation consulting is effectively unclaimed. The Big Four have AI practices built on outdated playbooks and no proprietary agent infrastructure. The window is 24–36 months.

KREW.CLOUD Solutions captures that window by combining human expertise with 300 KREW agents at internal cost. The leverage ratio is the business model: every human in the firm commands an agent squad, billing the output of a 200-person firm at the cost base of a 30-person boutique. Success fees — triggered after a documented 90-day measurement cycle — are expected to match consulting fee revenue by Year 3.

Why KREW.CLOUD Solutions is a separate company

KREW.CLOUD (the Virtual Office platform) and KREW.CLOUD Solutions (the consultancy) operate in the same market but through fundamentally different models. Keeping them separate preserves the integrity of both: the SaaS business scales without headcount; the consultancy deepens without platform constraints. Their synergies — agent supply, SaaS conversion, shared learning data, brand authority — are structural and compounding.

The ownership model

Spitsbergen Invest AS holds the controlling stake. The remaining 49.9% is distributed across three partnership tiers — Partner, Project Manager, Consultant — creating genuine ownership at every level of delivery. Partners are admitted by unanimous peer vote and board approval. They bring a client book. This is the entry requirement, not a negotiable nicety.

The 30 + 300 formula. 30 humans direct 300 KREW agents. Each agent squad costs less than one analyst hire. Every project bills human rates plus bundled agent capacity at a margin no conventional firm can approach. The firm is profitable from its first large engagement — it does not need to scale before it earns.

The structural advantage — founding Partner sourcing

The founding Partners are recruited from the senior ranks of international management consulting — former practitioners from McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Kearney, or equivalent Norwegian top-tier firms. They arrive with established client networks across Norwegian and Nordic industry, proven track records in operational and organisational transformation, and the commercial credibility to walk into a boardroom and close a mandate on the strength of their name alone. Network is the entry requirement. Brand presence follows results. This is not the profile of a typical startup hire — it is the profile of someone who has already built the thing KREW.CLOUD Solutions is now selling.

Why the Big Four cannot defend this market

  • No proprietary agent infrastructure. Their AI practices are built on tool-implementation playbooks — recommending third-party software, not deploying owned agents. KREW.CLOUD Solutions deploys 300 agents from Day 1.
  • High cost base. A Big Four AI team in Norway runs at NOK 25,000–40,000/day per partner equivalent. KREW Solutions human rates are comparable — but agent capacity is bundled in at internal cost. The margin structure cannot be matched.
  • No success-fee model. Established firms bill for input, not outcome. KREW Solutions ties a meaningful portion of revenue to measured results. That changes the client relationship entirely.
  • No learning loop. A Big Four team's knowledge disperses after the engagement. KREW's agent performance data feeds back into the Learning Loop — every client makes the platform better for the next one.

Section 4 · Service Architecture

What we sell, and how we price it

Owner: CPO (Amanda) · pricing reviewed by CFO (Bud) · CLO (Louis) on contract structure

KREW.CLOUD Solutions sells four defined service lines. Each has a bounded scope, a structured delivery model, and a pricing mechanism. All engagements include an agent-squad component sourced from KREW.CLOUD at internal transfer pricing. Clients do not manage agents directly — that is the service.

REMAP — Process Discovery & Redesign
Full diagnostic of a client's knowledge-work processes. Map current state, identify agentic workstreams, design the future operating model with a validated business case. Delivered by a Partner and a dedicated agent squad over 6–10 weeks.

Fee: NOK 600K – 1.5M per engagement
WIRE — Agent Deployment & Integration
Technical and operational implementation of the redesigned model. KREW agents configured, trained on client context (L4 Enterprise Layer), integrated with existing systems. Delivered by a PM-led team with a dedicated KREW agent squad.

Fee: NOK 1M – 4M per engagement
VERIFY — Measurement & ROI Tracking
90-day post-implementation performance cycle. Measures actual vs. projected savings, output quality, and adoption rates. Produces the documented evidence base for success fee calculation. No success fee is claimed without a completed VERIFY engagement.

Fee: NOK 200K – 500K · Gates success fee
ANCHOR — Ongoing Agent Management
Monthly retainer for agent performance monitoring, prompt optimisation, model updates, and quarterly business reviews. Converts project revenue to recurring. Creates the natural handoff path to a full KREW.CLOUD SaaS subscription.

Retainer: NOK 50K – 200K / month
Fifth revenue stream — KREW.CLOUD product commission. Solutions is the exclusive sales team for KREW.CLOUD products in the Norwegian market. After each WIRE engagement, it converts the client onto two KREW.CLOUD products and earns a profit share on those sales. Solutions takes all of it. This is income — entirely separate from the internal fleet, which is a P&L cost.

Commission: 50% of KREW.CLOUD gross profit for the first 3 months of every agent or worker onboarded to a client.
Loyalty bonus: 50% of one month's gross profit for every agent or worker still active at month 18.

Section 5 · Partnership & Ownership

Who owns it, and how the firm works

Owner: CLO (Louis) · Chairman (Andy Ombustvedt, Spitsbergen Invest AS)

KREW.CLOUD Solutions is structured as a tight partnership pyramid. Spitsbergen Invest AS holds the controlling stake. The remaining 49.9% is distributed across two equity-bearing tiers — Partner and Project Manager. Agentic Engineers (the execution layer) are salaried with an annual bonus pool tied to firm performance. No equity. A tighter pyramid with deeper per-head ownership at the top creates better alignment and faster decision-making than a diluted wide structure.

Profit distribution waterfall

Pre-tax profits are distributed in three tranches in the following fixed order. There is no discretion on sequence — the waterfall runs in full before any equity distribution is made.

30% — Bonus pool   Inclusive of all employer tax and social security contributions. Split evenly across all 30 human employees regardless of tier. At Year 3 EBITDA (NOK 153M) this equals NOK 1.53M per person — competitive at every level of the pyramid. Year 1: ~NOK 379K/person. Year 2: ~NOK 925K/person. Year 3: ~NOK 1.53M/person.

20% — Working capital reserve   Retained in the firm. Not distributed. Board-vote required to draw down below this floor. Protects against the 90–120 day success fee receivables lag and covers operating cash between project completions. Year 3: NOK 30.7M.

50% — Equity holder distributions   Distributed to shareholders pro-rata by ownership. At Year 3 this represents approximately NOK 76.6M available to equity holders. Spitsbergen Invest AS receives its 50.1% share; Partners and PMs receive per their equity pool allocations.
Employer tax (arbeidsgiveravgift) and any social contributions on the bonus pool are included within the 30% allocation — the firm bears these costs from within the pool, not on top of it. Net-to-employee bonus per person at Year 3 will be approximately NOK 1.1–1.2M after standard Norwegian tax.

Path to partner

  • Agentic Engineer → Project Manager: delivery track record across at least two engagements + one positive client reference + peer recommendation from a Partner. Board approval required.
  • Project Manager → Partner: demonstrated revenue generation + full partner endorsement (unanimous) + board approval. Partners must bring an active client book. They are admitted for their ability to grow the firm, not their delivery excellence alone.
  • Agent fluency is a gate at every level. Every promotion requires demonstrated ability to configure and direct a KREW agent squad. This is not optional. The leverage model only works if human leads can orchestrate agents. An Agentic Engineer who cannot run a squad independently is not a candidate for PM.

Exit provisions

  • Departing partners sell equity back at a formula valuation: 4× trailing 12-month revenue contribution.
  • No drag-along without board approval from Spitsbergen Invest AS.
  • Non-compete: 12 months, Norwegian market, AI consulting services — applies to all tiers.
  • Agentic Engineer bonus pool forfeit on voluntary exit mid-year unless board waives.

Managing Partner

One Partner is designated Managing Partner by board vote, renewable annually. The Managing Partner has day-to-day authority within board-approved strategy and budget. The Chairman (Spitsbergen Invest AS) may intervene on any matter at any time.

Full loyalty is the price of admission. Partners, Project Managers and Agentic Engineers may not engage with any competing agentic transformation activity that excludes or undermines KREW.CLOUD or KREW.CLOUD Solutions in any way. This is a condition of employment, equity, and continued membership in the firm.

The commission income described in Section 4 is separate and unrelated. How agent squads are deployed

Each engagement receives a dedicated squad of 5–15 agents drawn from the KREW.CLOUD roster — typically a mix of CFO, CLO, CTO, CMO, and specialist roles selected for the workstream. The Project Manager is the human-agent interface: they orchestrate agent work, own output quality, and manage the client relationship day-to-day. Clients do not manage agents directly. The squad composition changes per engagement; the management model does not.

Appendix · B · Internal Review

Business Developer review — KREW-BOARD-005

Author: Business Developer (Bruce) · KREW-A011 · commissioned by Chairman

This is a candid internal review of the Solutions Business Plan v1.0. I have reviewed it against market conditions, execution risk, and internal capability. My recommendation is at the bottom. Read the flags first.

What the plan gets right

  • The pyramid tightening is the right call. Four senior MC-background partners with deep networks and board-level credibility is a stronger commercial engine than eight mixed-background partners with shallow pipelines. Quality of network beats quantity of headcount every time in premium consulting. This decision is correct and should be held firm under hiring pressure.
  • No consultant equity is defensible at this stage. The Agentic Engineer role is a technical execution function, not an origination role. Equity at this tier would dilute the pyramid without adding proportional commercial value. A well-designed bonus pool is the right instrument here — better alignment, less governance complexity, easier to adjust annually as the firm scales.
  • The commission model is now correctly understood and it is stronger than the prior version. Each converted 200-employee client generates NOK 268,760 in commission over 18 months — and NOK 5.2M in potential success fee on the same transaction. These two income streams are earned from the same WIRE engagement, through different mechanisms, at near-zero marginal cost. At Year 3 with 18 client conversions, commission income alone is NOK 4.2M with a growing loyalty tail. The intercompany SLA (F5) must define gross profit for both Agentic Workers and VoA agents separately — the GP margins differ and must be agreed before the first conversion is executed.
  • The mandate-before-signing requirement for founding partners is the single most important governance discipline in this document. It ensures the firm has revenue before it has cost. Do not waive this for Partner #1 under any circumstances, regardless of how strong their CV reads. A signed mandate is the proof of network. A CV is not.
  • The 30+300 leverage ratio is real and defensible. No comparable firm in Norway can field 300 specialist agents at internal cost from Day 1. This is a genuine structural advantage. The plan correctly identifies it as the business model, not just a feature.
  • Success fees with a documented VERIFY gate are the right structure. Outcome-based fees change the client relationship from vendor to partner. The VERIFY gate protects the firm from disputed claims. Both of these will matter when the first large success fee is on the table.
