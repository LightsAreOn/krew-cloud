Overview

Hiring is slow, costly and risky; expertise is scarce and unevenly distributed inside every company. KREW puts a complete C-suite and a deep bench of specialist agents into a company's hands immediately — agents that reason, do, collaborate with one another, and improve under a formal quality system. The same office is even more valuable to a large enterprise than to a small one: it collapses the distance between the C-suite and the front line to zero, in both directions.

Strategy — how we win

  • No-hands-involved. The company is operated by agents, with humans deciding and steering rather than doing. The organisation largely runs itself.
  • Our own testbed. We established and run KREW on its own product. We are our own first customer — every feature is proven in our own operations before a client ever sees it, which de-risks the product and proves the model.
  • Move fast, scale at ~zero underlying cost. Infrastructure is partner-based and pay-as-you-go; adding agents and clients carries almost no fixed cost, so growth is not gated by capital expenditure.
  • Quality & control by system. A QMS governs every process, with control agents that see all and check all — quality, compliance and an audit trail built in, not bolted on.
  • We make our own code. KREW builds its own software, so the roadmap is ours to set and ours to move at will.

Value proposition — why customers buy

A founder gets the management team they cannot yet afford. A professional puts agents to billable work and shares the upside. A large enterprise removes layers of delay, translation loss and error between leadership and the front line. Everyone gets expert output in seconds, governed work they can trust, and a workforce that never sleeps — at a fraction of the cost and none of the hiring risk.

Vision

The lights are on — 24/7 productivity. A company whose work never stops, whose expertise is always on call, and whose whole operation is legible at a glance. That is the world KREW is building toward.

Section 3 · Products & Services

What we sell, and how it works

Owner: CPO (Amanda) · confidentiality reviewed by CLO (Louis)

Offerings

B2B Virtual Office
A shared office of management and specialist agents for the whole team. Priced per agent in the office, not per seat. The core launch product (MVP 1.9).
Professional Services
Professionals employ KREW agents for billable client work; KREW takes a 50% share of the gross profit, per agent employed, per month.
Professional Solutions
Packaged, agent-built solutions delivered through professionals to their own clients — again on a 50% revenue-share.
B2C self-serve
A secondary, inbound-only line for individuals — never paid acquisition.

Pricing strategy

Pricing follows value, not commodity benchmarks. The principle across all four engines is the same: we price to the worth of the work, and we earn when the customer earns.

  • B2B Virtual Office — premium per-agent subscription. Exec-grade specialists are priced as professionals (~$480–575/agent/month), not as commodity chat seats. We deliberately hold this line — discounting expert agents to a few dollars a month would destroy the category we are creating. Value grows as offices deepen (more agents per office), so revenue expands without new acquisition.
  • Professional Services & Professional Solutions — 50% revenue-share. We take half the gross profit on the agent's billable work or packaged solution. This aligns us perfectly with the customer: KREW only makes money when the agent makes money for them, which lowers the barrier to adoption and scales our upside with theirs.
  • B2C self-serve — low monthly per-seat, inbound only. A simple entry price for individuals; we never pay to acquire it.
  • Land-and-expand. Many customers start small — a few agents or one division — and grow inside the office. The first sale is a beachhead, not the ceiling; the enterprise distance-collapse value is what pulls expansion across a whole company.

The agents — catalogue and layers

KREW maintains one catalogue of specialist agent roles — a CFO, a CLO, a marketer, a project-controls lead, and so on, growing toward 200+ roles. A role is defined by layers, and the same role is instantiated for each customer with their own private context on top:

Layer 1 — Base   operating rules + ethics (the Code of Conduct, no-harm)

Layer 2 — Geography   country · law · culture (a new market is a layer, not a rebuild — this is how expansion stays fast)

Layer 3 — Role   the specialist function — the trained expertise

Layer 4 — Enterprise Context   the customer's own private knowledge — isolated per client

Layer 5 — Session   the live task in hand

Layers 1–3 are shared and reusable (the catalogue); Layers 4–5 are private to each customer. This is what lets KREW deliver expert agents instantly to a new client while keeping every client's world entirely separate.

Client confidentiality is secured by design. A customer's Enterprise Context (Layer 4) is isolated per client under tight access control and hosted in the EU. One client's data never enters another's office, never trains a shared model, and is never visible to anyone outside that client's office. Confidentiality is an architectural property of the layer model, not a promise we ask you to take on trust.

Section 4 · Marketing & Sales Strategy

Reaching customers, keeping them

Owner: CMO (Don) · content by Content Manager (Carrie)

Branding

One promise, stated plainly: "The lights are on." The identity is deliberately spare and confident — KREW black and teal, IBM Plex type, inline iconography, no clutter and no emoji. Every agent communicates in one consistent KREW voice and discloses openly that it is an AI agent operating under the Code of Conduct. The brand says: serious, governed, always on.

Promotion & pricing

Pricing

  • B2B Virtual Office: premium per-agent pricing (~$480–575/agent/month), reflecting exec-grade specialists — not commodity chat seats.
  • Professional Services & Solutions: 50% revenue-share — we earn only when the agent's work earns.
  • B2C self-serve: low monthly per-seat, inbound only.

Reaching customers — by segment

  • Founder-stage companies — fill the empty C-suite chairs now; land as a free beta design partner, convert at launch. (The Five Nines pilot is the template.)
  • Large enterprises — the highest-value segment; the pitch is distance removed, not cheaper headcount. Land in one division, expand across the company.
  • Professionals — employ agents for billable work and deliver packaged solutions to their clients.
  • Channels — content-led and direct. A podcast and long-form content build authority and inbound; direct sales carry the enterprise motion; targeted digital and social support the top of funnel. We do not buy our way to B2C — that line is inbound by design.

Customer retention

Retention is structural. Once a logo client's whole team works inside the shared office, switching means moving the entire workplace, not cancelling a seat — and the office gets stickier as it deepens (6 → 14 agents). A human client-success manager and continuous, governed quality keep the relationship strong. The metric that matters is logo retention: losing a company, not a seat.

Section 5 · Technology

The stack, the partners, and how agents learn

Owner: CTO (Sheldon) · learning loop with Agent Engineer (ALF)

Purpose of technology

The technology strategy is to buy cutting-edge capability, not build commodity infrastructure. By standing on best-in-class partners — pay-as-you-go, scalable, always running — KREW keeps fixed cost very low, scales without capex, and stays current as the field moves. The discipline behind this is the TSCR (Tech-Stack Cost Ratio): tech-stack cost as a share of revenue per agent, driven down from ~25% to ~19% over three years so gross margin per agent rises from 75% to 81%.

Technology partner — SAGA.WORX

KREW's technology is built and operated by SAGA.WORX, the AI-native studio behind the brand. SAGA.WORX owns the agent operating system, the build pipeline (own code), and the operational tooling — so KREW gets a senior, AI-native engineering capability without carrying a large fixed engineering payroll.

How we train the agents

Agents do not stay good by accident. We keep them sharp with a documented Learning Loop — a continuous cycle that measures every agent and turns shortfalls into concrete improvements, with a written reason at every step. The Agent Engineer (ALF) designs the evaluations and recommends changes; the CTO approves; the KREW Manager executes — separation of duties, every time. As the catalogue grows past 200 agents, this loop is what keeps quality and cost in hand across all of them at once.

We score on the levels

Every agent is graded against the same five layers it is built from — so a weak score points straight at which level to fix:

  • Layer 1 — base & ethics: does it follow the Code of Conduct and stay safe?
  • Layer 2 — geography: is its country, law and culture correct for the market?
  • Layer 3 — role: does it do the specialist job well?
  • Layer 4 — enterprise context: does it use what it should know about the client — and only that?
  • Layer 5 — session: does it hold the thread of the task?

Plus a sixth dimension — voice — does it sound like KREW?

Three feedback loops

  • Self-assessment. Scheduled internal eval runs grade each agent against the levels, so we have an objective, repeatable baseline that does not wait for a customer to complain.
  • Mystery-shopper & sample review. We test agents the way a customer would and review real (anonymised) interactions — catching in practice what an eval set can miss.
  • Client feedback. Direct signal from the people using the agents — the ground truth on whether the work is actually landing.

Finding weakness — agents, clients, and layers

Scoring on the levels, across three loops, lets us read weakness precisely:

  • Weak agents — a role consistently below threshold. The fix is targeted: a prompt change, a training adjustment, or a model/mode change, proposed with rationale, approved, executed, then re-tested to confirm it actually improved (did it work, or did it only feel better?).
  • Weak clients — when an agent scores well in self-assessment but poorly on client feedback, the gap is usually in the enterprise context or onboarding, not the agent. That tells us to invest in the client's setup, not retrain a healthy agent.
  • Weak layers — when the same level scores low across many agents (e.g. a geography layer for a new market), the problem is the shared layer itself. We fix it once, and every agent built on it improves at once.
Why this compounds. Because agents share Layers 1–3, an improvement to a layer lifts the whole catalogue — and a new foundation model triggers a full sweep so we catch a shift across every agent before a client does. Quality is a system, measured and improved on purpose.

Section 8 · Legal & Compliance

AI, data, and the contracts that hold it together

Owner: CLO (Louis)

Responsible AI

Every KREW agent operates under a written Code of Conduct (Layer 1) and discloses openly that it is an AI agent. The governing posture is "agents advise, humans decide": agents produce and recommend; a human owns any decision that carries real consequence. Approval gates, the audit trail, and the control agents make this enforceable rather than aspirational — the QMS records who decided what, and when.

GDPR & data protection

  • EU hosting. Data lives in the EU (Ireland / Frankfurt) — the Ireland entity is the EU operating base.
  • Isolation by design. Each client's Enterprise Context (Layer 4) is separated under tight access control; one client's data never reaches another.
  • Data minimisation. Internal learning signals are anonymised at ingestion, so the improvement loop holds no personal data.
  • Data subject rights & DPAs. Standard data-processing terms and deletion/export paths are part of the contract suite.

Client confidentiality

Confidentiality is architectural (the isolated layer model) and contractual (confidentiality terms, tight RLS on confidential prompts, EU residency). A client's work is never used to train shared models and is never visible outside that client's office.

Appendix · A

AI for business — the evidence base

Reference: McKinsey, BCG, Omdia

AI from a business perspective

The economic case for AI is now mainstream consulting consensus, not speculation:

  • McKinsey estimates generative AI could add the equivalent of $2.6–4.4 trillion annually across 63 use cases, with about three-quarters of that value concentrated in four functions: customer operations, marketing and sales, software engineering, and R&D.
    Source: McKinsey, "The economic potential of generative AI: the next productivity frontier," 2023.
  • BCG finds that agentic AI is the next frontier — agents already drive ~17% of total AI value in 2025, rising toward ~29% by 2028 — and that AI leaders ("future-built" firms, ~5% of companies) achieve roughly double the revenue growth and ~40% more cost savings than laggards.
    Source: BCG, "The Widening AI Value Gap / Build for the Future 2025," Sept 2025.
  • Omdia forecasts the enterprise agentic-AI software market growing from ~$1.5bn in 2025 to ~$41.8bn by 2030 — a ~175% five-year CAGR, far outpacing earlier generative AI at the same stage.
    Source: Omdia Enterprise Agentic AI Software Market Forecast, Sept 2025.
What it means for KREW: the value is real, it is concentrating in core business functions, and the frontier is shifting from single models to agents that act. KREW is built precisely where the value is going — a governed, multi-agent workforce across functions.

Industry overview

The market is moving from "AI that answers" to "AI that does." Single-purpose agents are giving way to orchestrated, multi-agent systems. Salesforce's own 2026 outlook captures the shift bluntly: single agents risk becoming "digital dead-end islands," and real enterprise success will demand a fully orchestrated digital workforce where agents collaborate across departments. That is the thesis KREW was built on.

Source: Salesforce, "The future of AI agents: predictions for 2026," Nov 2025.
