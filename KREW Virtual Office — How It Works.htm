Core Premise

The Virtual Office is not software. It is an operating model enforced by software. Humans make the decisions that require genuine judgment. Agents handle everything else — not when asked, but the moment it is needed. The quality of the Virtual Office is measured by one number: how few decisions the Chairman needs to make per week, and how good those decisions are when made.

Design Brief & Objectives

The Problem We Are Solving

Every company — from a PE portfolio company to a Norwegian SME founder — loses most of its operating capacity to latency. Latency between a lead arriving and someone responding. Between a contract being signed and onboarding starting. Between a compliance deadline appearing and a lawyer starting. Between a meeting request arriving and a calendar being checked. These gaps are not failures of effort — they are structural. The company is designed around human bandwidth.

KREW is not about replacing humans. It is about eliminating the latency that exists between events and human action, in the hours when humans are not working, and on the tasks that do not require human judgment. A company running on KREW has agents covering the gaps. The Chairman makes decisions. Agents execute everything else — continuously, correctly, and with a full audit trail.

What Is Not In Scope for 11.11

Multi-user shared workspaces. BPR consulting. Custom integrations requiring code. Mobile-native app. Public API. These are post-launch. The 11.11 product is: one Chairman, nine agents, one Virtual Office, one brief every morning, one Decision Queue, and a set of integrations that mean work starts automatically. That is enough to prove the model.

The Operating Model

Three-Tier Authority

Every action in the Virtual Office is assigned to one of three authority tiers. The tier determines whether an agent acts autonomously, presents to the Chairman for approval, or waits for a Chairman instruction. This is the Human-in-the-Loop architecture. It is also the EU AI Act compliance framework.

T1
Agent Autonomous

Agent executes without Chairman approval. Full audit log after. No external consequence.

Draft documents · Internal analysis · Task card management · Brief generation · QMS entries · Research
T2
Decision Queue

Agent prepares complete recommended action. Chairman reviews and approves with one tap. Nothing external happens until approved.

External emails · Client communications · Partner replies · Content publication · Scheduling commitments
T3
Chairman Initiates

No agent action until Chairman gives explicit instruction. Legal, financial, or reputational consequence.

Contract signing · Financial commitments · Board decisions · External legal counsel · Investor communications
EU AI Act Mapping — Article 14 (Human Oversight)

T1 actions are fully documented in qms_audit_log with agent identity, timestamp, and output. T2 records the Chairman's approval decision with timestamp before external action. T3 records the initiation instruction. Together, these three tiers provide the complete human oversight audit trail required by EU AI Act Article 14 for high-risk AI system deployment. Monica (QSM) owns this register.

Workflow W-02 — The Decision Queue

The Decision Queue is the primary product from the Chairman's perspective. Every T2 action lands here — fully prepared by the relevant agent, ready for a single approval or rejection. Nothing external happens without a Chairman decision recorded in the audit log. This is the interface between agent intelligence and human authority.

1

Event detected

Trigger / AgentAn event occurs that requires a Chairman action: an email needs a reply, a lead arrives, a contract needs review, a board meeting requires confirmation, a compliance item needs sign-off.

2

Agent prepares the complete action

Donna / Relevant AgentThe responsible agent researches context (BRREG, calendar, history, prior correspondence), prepares the full recommended action — drafted reply, calendar block, compliance note, financial model — and adds a rationale note explaining why this action is recommended and what the consequence of not acting is.

3

Suggestion delivered to Chairman

Donna PACurrent mode: Donna sends a WO Suggestion email to Chairman inbox containing the full draft, calendar details, and a two-step action guide. Future mode (KREW TEAM): appears as a card in the Decision Queue UI. One-tap Approve or Reject with reason.

4

Chairman approves or rejects

Chairman · T2 DecisionChairman reads the suggestion and taps Approve or Reject. Approval is logged with timestamp and Chairman attribution. Rejection includes a reason that feeds back to the agent for calibration. This decision record is the HITL compliance artefact.

5

Agent executes the approved action

Agent · Post-ApprovalOnly after approval: the external action fires. Email is sent. Calendar event is created. Document is published. The task card moves to Done. Monica logs the outcome in the QMS. If rejected, the agent notes the feedback and the task returns to backlog for rework.

Workflow W-05 — The Donna WO Suggestion Pattern

When a Chairman Work Order is identified, Donna does not flag it and wait. She prepares the complete suggested action before the Chairman knows it needs doing. By the time it appears in the brief or Decision Queue, all the Chairman needs to do is read, approve, and the action fires.

1

Identify the obligation type

Donna PAClassify: reply required, attendance required, decision required, payment required, signature required, approval required. Mandatory obligations (board membership, legal deadlines, regulatory) are flagged as MANDATORY — not discretionary.

2

Research context

Donna PACheck all available sources: BRREG for board membership and legal obligations, calendar for availability, prior correspondence for tone and relationship history, QMS for related task cards, email log for relevant recent threads.

3

Prepare the specific action

Donna PAFor a reply: draft the complete email in the correct language and register. For a meeting: calculate drive time, prepare calendar block with buffer. For a decision: prepare a one-paragraph brief with recommendation and consequence of inaction. The draft must be ready to send — not a framework for writing one.

4

Deliver to Decision Queue

Donna PA → ChairmanCurrent: WO Suggestion email to ao@spitsbergeninvest.com with full draft, calendar block, and two-step action guide. Future (KREW TEAM + Graph API): Donna creates the Outlook draft directly and adds the calendar event. Chairman taps Approve once.

Workflow W-07 — Quality & Compliance (Monica · ISO 9001)

Compliance in KREW is not a workstream. It is a byproduct of the system running correctly. Every agent action writes to qms_audit_log. Every Decision Queue item records a Chairman decision. Every task card tracks from proposed to done with timestamps and attribution. Monica's role is to verify this is happening, flag when it is not, and ensure the audit trail is complete.

ISO 9001:2015 Clause Mapping

ISO 9001:2015 · Clause 7.5 — Documented Information
All KREW process outputs are documented automatically

qms_audit_log provides the documented information record required by 7.5. Every agent action, Decision Queue decision, and task state change is recorded with creator, timestamp, and content. Monica maintains the QMS document register (qms_documents table). No manual filing required — the system files itself.

ISO 9001:2015 · Clause 8.5 — Production and Service Provision
The three-tier HITL model ensures controlled conditions

Tier 1 (autonomous) actions are controlled by the agent's authority scope — defined in KREW-OPS-015. Tier 2 (Decision Queue) adds a Chairman approval gate. Tier 3 (Chairman initiates) adds an initiation record. Together these constitute the "controlled conditions" required by 8.5 for all service provision actions.

ISO 9001:2015 · Clause 9.1 — Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis
ALF evaluations and Monica QMS reviews are the monitoring programme

ALF runs quarterly eval sweeps across all agents. Monica reviews task card quality weekly. The KREW Learning Loop (KREW-PROD-014) documents how shortfalls are identified, proposed, approved by CTO, and executed. Calibration data feeds back into agent prompts on a documented schedule.

ISO 9001:2015 · Clause 10.2 — Nonconformity and Corrective Action
Deviations are registered, investigated, and resolved within the QMS

Monica registers nonconformities in qms_audit_log (kind='deviation'). Each deviation has a root cause note, corrective action proposed, and verification status. The agent executor's FLAG outputs are the primary deviation signal — when an agent cannot proceed, that is a potential process gap requiring investigation.

EU AI Act — Article 14 (Human Oversight) Compliance

The three-tier authority model directly satisfies Article 14. T1: agent actions are fully logged with agent identity. T2: Chairman approval is recorded before external action. T3: Chairman initiation is recorded before any action. Monica maintains the Article 14 compliance register as a QMS-tracked document (KREW-GOV-003, in preparation).

The Guarantee

The Chairman receives the brief at 08:00 every morning regardless of what happened overnight. If the 01:00 pipeline ran perfectly, the 08:00 brief confirms it. If the 01:00 pipeline failed for any reason, the 08:00 watchdog re-runs the full pipeline and sends the brief anyway. Arrival of the email at 08:00 is the system health signal. If it does not arrive by 08:15, something is wrong at the infrastructure level — not the pipeline.

Integration Architecture

Integrations are how events in the world enter the Virtual Office automatically. The goal is that every relevant event — a lead, a GitHub issue, a signed contract, a booked meeting — fires an agent without human initiation. The 64 Slack connectors available in sagaworx.slack.com are the integration surface.
